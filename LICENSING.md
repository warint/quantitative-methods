# Licensing, attribution and citation

This repository is deliberately licensed in **two parts**, because it contains two different kinds
of work. Creative Commons licences are written for expressive material and say nothing about source
code, patents or warranty; software licences say nothing about how to credit a lecture. Using one
licence for both would leave a gap on one side.

| What | Licence | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Teaching materials** | [CC BY-NC 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/) — see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) | All `*.md` files: `README.md`, `SYLLABUS.md`, `RESEARCH-MANDATES.md`, `GROUP-ASSESSMENT.md`, every `00-pre-session/`, `01-lecture/`, `02-practice/` page, `assessments/`, rubrics, templates |
| **Code** | [MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit) — see [`LICENSE-CODE`](LICENSE-CODE) | `scripts/`, every `**/starter/` file, any `*.py`, `*.ipynb`, `requirements.txt`, `.vscode/` configuration |
| **Data spine** | CC BY-NC 4.0, as teaching material | `data/spine/*.parquet` and its dictionaries — these are **synthetic fixtures** generated by `scripts/build_spine/make_fixtures.py` (seed 60033), not observed data |

## What CC BY-NC 4.0 asks of you

You may copy, adapt, translate, re-teach and redistribute these materials, provided you:

1. **credit the author** — name, a link to this repository, and a link to the licence;
2. **indicate whether you changed anything**, and keep any notice that a change was made;
3. **do not imply endorsement** — attribution is not the author's approval of your version;
4. **do not use them commercially** — see below for where that line falls.

### What "non-commercial" rules out, and what it does not

The licence defines non-commercial as *not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial
advantage or monetary compensation*. In practice, for material of this kind:

**Fine.** Teaching this course, or any part of it, at a university, college or school, including one
that charges tuition. Translating it. Posting your adaptation publicly for others to teach from.
Using it to learn, or to prepare a class you are paid a salary to give. Citing it in research.

**Not fine without asking.** Selling it, or selling access to it. Packaging it into a paid course, a
paid bootcamp, or a subscription product. Using it as the content of commercial corporate training.
Bundling it with a product you sell.

If you want to do something in the second column, ask — permission is often given, and a licence
covering it can be granted separately. Note also that the **code is MIT** and carries no such
restriction; only the teaching materials are non-commercial.

The licence is irrevocable, but it also lets the author **request removal of their attribution**
from an adaptation. If you build something you would rather not have associated with this course,
that request may come.

## Exceptions — material that is not the author's to license

- **Quoted readings, figures and excerpts.** Where a session page quotes a paper, book or report,
  that excerpt remains under its own copyright and is included under fair dealing / fair use for
  teaching. It is not relicensed by this repository.
- **Live source data.** `scripts/build_spine/build.py` can replace the synthetic spine with real
  data from Eurostat, UN Comtrade and the ECB. Those sources carry **incompatible terms** — Eurostat
  permits reuse with attribution, UN Comtrade restricts redistribution outright. Data fetched this
  way are **not** covered by this repository's licences and are excluded by `.gitignore` on purpose.
  Do not commit them.
- **Student work.** Anything under `**/groups/A2026/group-XX/` belongs to its authors.

## How to cite

The licence makes credit a legal condition; a citation makes it a scholarly one. GitHub reads
[`CITATION.cff`](CITATION.cff) and renders a *Cite this repository* button with BibTeX and APA
output. In text:

> Warin, T. (2026). *Quantitative Methods in International Business with Python* [Course materials].
> CC BY-NC 4.0. https://github.com/warint/quantitative-methods

BibTeX:

```bibtex
@misc{warin2026math60033a,
  author       = {Warin, Thierry},
  title        = {Quantitative Methods in International Business with Python},
  year         = {2026},
  howpublished = {Course materials, CC BY-NC 4.0},
  url          = {https://github.com/warint/quantitative-methods}
}
```

The canonical URL is <https://github.com/warint/quantitative-methods>.

## One thing to check before publishing

Universities frequently claim ownership of, or a standing licence in, course materials produced as
part of teaching duties. A licence can only grant what the licensor holds. Confirm your
institution's intellectual-property policy on course materials before making this repository public.
This file describes common practice; it is not legal advice.
