> ### What this paper is testing, and why
>
> The paper tests your **understanding** of the methods: what a number means, and what it does not
> license. There is very little arithmetic. The six parts test six different things:
>
> **Part I — Europe 2031.** Can you think clearly about what AI is, and about a scenario, in words?
>
> **Part II — the reading.** Can you state a paper's question, describe its model, criticise it, and
> name another way to answer it?
>
> **Part A — multiple choice.** Do you hold the core ideas of sessions 2 to 5 precisely?
>
> **Part B — short answers.** Can you state a concept in two or three exact sentences?
>
> **Part C — reading output.** Given a table, can you say what it shows — and nothing more?
>
> **Part D — judgement.** Knowing what a number does *not* license. The hardest part, and the one
> that separates an analyst from a technician.

# Part I — Europe 2031

**15 points · answer in words, drawing on the scenario and on our Session 1 conversation, *what AI
actually is* · no calculation**

*Europe 2031* (ARQ Foundation, June 2026) follows Europe through a period of rapid AI progress
between 2026 and 2031. Its prologue (January 2025 – June 2026) is drawn from the observed record;
its main narrative (August 2026 – March 2031) is constructed. By 2031 it gives the United States
255.1 GW of AI compute and Europe 20.9 GW.

**I(a).** *(3)* What is the difference between a **scenario** and a **forecast**? Where is the seam
between the fact-based and the speculative parts of *Europe 2031*, and why can the figures 255.1 GW
and 20.9 GW not be cited as predictions?

<br><br><br>

**I(b).** *(4)* In Session 1 you downloaded a language model of about four gigabytes. **What is in
that file**, and **what computation runs** when it answers a question? Is it fair to say that it
"understands" the subject it writes about?

<br><br><br>

**I(c).** *(4)* Which of the methods in this course would you call **"AI"**, and which are
statistical methods that are more than a century old? Give two examples of each. Does the
distinction matter for how much you should trust a result? Explain.

<br><br><br>

**I(d).** *(4)* The scenario contrasts **"compute sovereignty"** — Europe building its own AI
capacity — with **"governed interdependence"** — working with US providers while keeping critical
infrastructure and rules under European law. Which is the better objective for Europe? Argue a
position, with one argument against it.

# Part A — Multiple choice

**30 points · 20 questions × 1.5 · tick one answer per question · no penalty for a wrong answer**

**1.** *(S2)* The standard deviation of household income in a sample is 12,000. In what unit is it expressed?

- [ ] **A.** percent
- [ ] **B.** euros
- [ ] **C.** euros squared
- [ ] **D.** it has no unit

**2.** *(S2)* Why does the sample variance divide by $n - 1$ rather than $n$?

- [ ] **A.** to make the variance larger, as a safety margin
- [ ] **B.** because one observation is always an outlier
- [ ] **C.** because the mean was estimated from the same data, so only $n - 1$ deviations are free
- [ ] **D.** because the first observation is dropped

**3.** *(S2)* A variable is symmetric but has much heavier tails than a normal distribution. The 68–95–99.7 rule:

- [ ] **A.** applies exactly, because the variable is symmetric
- [ ] **B.** applies to the median only
- [ ] **C.** applies once the outliers are removed
- [ ] **D.** may fail: it holds for a roughly normal variable

**4.** *(S2)* The median of 3, 7, 8, 12 and 40 is:

- [ ] **A.** 8
- [ ] **B.** 14
- [ ] **C.** 12
- [ ] **D.** 7

**5.** *(S3)* Ordinary least squares chooses the line that:

- [ ] **A.** minimises the sum of squared vertical distances between the points and the line
- [ ] **B.** passes through the largest number of points
- [ ] **C.** maximises the slope
- [ ] **D.** minimises the number of outliers

**6.** *(S3)* A regression of GDP per capita on a productivity index (observed between 72 and 129) has an intercept of −86,000. You should:

- [ ] **A.** report that a country with no productivity has negative income
- [ ] **B.** drop the intercept from the model
- [ ] **C.** not interpret it: it is a prediction far outside the data
- [ ] **D.** conclude the model is wrong

**7.** *(S3)* Exports and productivity are positively related across firms. Which of these is **not** a reason to doubt that productivity causes exports?

- [ ] **A.** exporting may raise productivity (reverse causality)
- [ ] **B.** management quality may raise both (a common cause)
- [ ] **C.** only successful firms survive into the sample (selection)
- [ ] **D.** the sample has 5,000 firms

**8.** *(S3)* A predictor has a variance inflation factor (VIF) of 15. This means:

- [ ] **A.** it has no effect
- [ ] **B.** it is almost a combination of the other predictors, so its coefficient is unstable
- [ ] **C.** its coefficient is biased upward
- [ ] **D.** the model's R² is 15%

**9.** *(S3)* Two models are compared with AIC and BIC on the same rows. Which criterion favours the **smaller** model more strongly?

- [ ] **A.** AIC
- [ ] **B.** BIC
- [ ] **C.** both equally
- [ ] **D.** neither: they only measure fit

**10.** *(S3)* Why is GDP per capita often entered in logarithms?

- [ ] **A.** to reduce its right skew and the pull of a few very rich countries
- [ ] **B.** to make all coefficients positive
- [ ] **C.** because logarithms raise R²
- [ ] **D.** because GDP cannot be negative

**11.** *(S4)* The odds of an event are 3. Its probability is:

- [ ] **A.** 0.33
- [ ] **B.** 0.25
- [ ] **C.** 0.75
- [ ] **D.** 3

**12.** *(S4)* A logistic regression is estimated by:

- [ ] **A.** minimising the squared residuals
- [ ] **B.** taking the mean of y in each group
- [ ] **C.** dropping the observations with y = 0
- [ ] **D.** maximum likelihood

**13.** *(S4)* In a logit, one more unit of x moves the **probability** the most when the predicted probability is near:

- [ ] **A.** 0
- [ ] **B.** 0.5
- [ ] **C.** 1
- [ ] **D.** the base rate

**14.** *(S4)* A missed default costs a bank far more than a false alarm. The threshold for flagging a borrower should be:

- [ ] **A.** below 0.5
- [ ] **B.** exactly 0.5
- [ ] **C.** above 0.5
- [ ] **D.** the base rate, always

**15.** *(S4)* A classifier has an AUC of 0.90. This measures:

- [ ] **A.** the share of correct predictions
- [ ] **B.** how close its probabilities are to observed frequencies
- [ ] **C.** how well it ranks positives above negatives
- [ ] **D.** its precision at the 0.5 threshold

**16.** *(S4)* Of the firms a model flags as "will fail", the share that actually fail is called the:

- [ ] **A.** recall
- [ ] **B.** accuracy
- [ ] **C.** base rate
- [ ] **D.** precision

**17.** *(S5)* As the penalty $\lambda$ of a ridge regression increases:

- [ ] **A.** bias rises and variance falls
- [ ] **B.** bias and variance both fall
- [ ] **C.** bias falls and variance rises
- [ ] **D.** nothing changes on new data

**18.** *(S5)* There are more predictors than observations. Which method still gives a unique solution?

- [ ] **A.** OLS
- [ ] **B.** OLS with all the predictors standardised
- [ ] **C.** ridge
- [ ] **D.** none

**19.** *(S5)* Why must predictors be standardised before a lasso?

- [ ] **A.** to make the coefficients positive
- [ ] **B.** because the penalty depends on the units in which each predictor is measured
- [ ] **C.** to remove outliers
- [ ] **D.** to raise R²

**20.** *(S5)* In 5-fold cross-validation, each observation is used to **test** the model:

- [ ] **A.** five times
- [ ] **B.** never
- [ ] **C.** four times
- [ ] **D.** exactly once

# Part C — Reading output

**15 points · 3 items × 5 · answer in words · no calculation beyond reading the table**

## C1. Two regressions *(5 points)*

The export share of 400 manufacturing firms (in % of sales) is regressed on a productivity index,
then on the same index and firm size (log of employees). One year, one country.

```
                         Model 1       Model 2
                         coef  p       coef  p
const                    -3.8  0.21   -12.4  0.001
productivity_idx         0.48  0.000   0.29  0.000
log_employees              —     —     2.10  0.000
R²                       0.21          0.30
```

**C1(a).** *(2)* Write the sentence a reader needs for the productivity coefficient in Model 2.

<br><br>

**C1(b).** *(1.5)* Why does the productivity coefficient fall from 0.48 to 0.29 when firm size is
added?

<br>

**C1(c).** *(1.5)* Model 2's intercept is −12.4. Should you interpret it? Explain.

<br>

## C2. A logistic regression *(5 points)*

Whether a firm has adopted AI (1) or not (0), on two characteristics.

```
                     coef (log-odds)    odds ratio e^coef
large_firm                1.10               3.00
does_rd                   0.70               2.01

Predicted probability of adoption      no R&D     R&D
small firm                              0.10      0.18
large firm                              0.25      0.40
```

**C2(a).** *(2)* Write a correct sentence using the odds ratio 3.00.

<br><br>

**C2(b).** *(2)* Doing R&D adds 8 percentage points to the probability of adoption for a small
firm, but 15 points for a large one, although its odds ratio is the same. Why?

<br><br>

**C2(c).** *(1)* A report says "large firms are three times more likely to adopt AI". Is that what the
table shows? Explain.

<br>

## C3. Choosing the penalty *(5 points)*

A lasso is fitted to predict R&D intensity from 40 candidate indicators, for five values of the
penalty $\lambda$.

```
λ                        0.001    0.01    0.1     1      10
error on training rows    0.80    0.85    0.95   1.20   1.60
cross-validation error    1.65    1.30    1.05   1.25   1.62
predictors kept             40      28      12      4      0
```

**C3(a).** *(2)* Which $\lambda$ would you choose, and why?

<br><br>

**C3(b).** *(2)* The training error rises steadily with $\lambda$, while the cross-validation error
first falls and then rises. Explain both, using the bias–variance trade-off.

<br><br>

**C3(c).** *(1)* What does the model at $\lambda = 10$ predict?

# Part D — Interpretation and judgement

**12 points · one paragraph · a well-argued refusal earns full marks**

An export-credit agency has trained a classifier that flags "high-risk" export markets from 30
country indicators. On past data its **AUC is 0.93**, and political instability is its most
important feature. An analyst proposes to **stop insuring all exports to the flagged countries**, and
writes that "the model proves that political instability causes export failure".

Write the paragraph you would send in reply. It must state: **what the AUC and the feature ranking
are** (3), **what they do not license**, and why (3), **what evidence would be needed** before acting
on or explaining the model this way (3), and **what you would recommend the agency do in the
meantime** (3).
