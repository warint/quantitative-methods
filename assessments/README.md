## 2. From a mark out of 100 to a letter

The four components add up to a mark out of 100. That mark is then **converted to a letter grade on
a curve, with the class median at B+**.

- The curve is applied **once**, at that conversion, never inside a component. Participation is
  scored against a published bar; the midterm and the papers are marked against their rubrics.
- It is not a quota of letters, and you are not competing with the person next to you: it sets the
  number-to-letter mapping against how the class performed on this particular set of assessments,
  so an exam that turned out harder than intended does not cost you a letter.

## 3. Participation — 10%

Earned by **sustained engagement across the term**, through any mix of three routes:

| Route | What counts |
|---|---|
| **In the room** | a question that changed how another group saw its result, a connection between two angles, a correction that landed |
| **In the practice** | working the ninety minutes with your group: your own commits, the two-minute report when drawn, engaging with other groups |
| **In the optional lab** | a completed [QMIB Lab](https://warin.ca/qmib-labs/) knowledge check after a session — counted for completion, never for correctness |

- **The bar is absolute, not a ranking.** It is published, and it is the same for everyone. The
  class distribution is used to calibrate it, never to assign a mark.
- Each student ends as **pass** (full marks), **review** (an instructor decision) or **fail** (a
  partial mark). Being quiet is not being absent: a student who worked steadily is flagged for
  review, never zeroed automatically.
- The one thing recorded against you: **not delivering the two-minute report when your group is
  drawn**.

Details: [`GROUP-ASSESSMENT.md`](../GROUP-ASSESSMENT.md#the-participation-grade).

## 4. Midterm exam — 30%

**Wednesday 28 October 2026 · 3 hours · on paper, in pen · a simple calculator · no notes, no
computer, no internet · Sessions 1 to 6.**

| Part | Points | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **A** · Definitions | 24 | stating a concept precisely — 8 questions × 3 |
| **B** · Calculations | 36 | deriving and computing, working shown — 6 problems × 6 |
| **C** · Diagnosis | 24 | reading an output and finding the planted fault — 3 items × 8 |
| **D** · Judgement | 16 | what a number does and does not license — 2 questions × 8 |
| | **100** | |

- **Working is marked.** A correct answer with no derivation earns part marks; a correct derivation
  with an arithmetic slip earns most of them.
- **A well-argued refusal earns full marks** in Part D.
- Practise on the [sample paper](midterm/EXAM.md) under exam conditions, then check it against the
  [worked solutions](midterm/SOLUTIONS.md). The [six-page summary](https://warint.github.io/quantitative-methods/midterm-summary.pdf)
  covers every session examined.

## 5. Team work — 20%

**The SDAfrique methods paper**: your practice group's method, carried from European data to a
problem in francophone Africa, written for a student in the SDAfrique programme who works in R.
The project and its **intellectual property belong to the SDAfrique team**: you provide
intelligence — analysis, method, evaluation — and you **do not code their software**
([details](team-work/README.md#whose-project-it-is)).

| Part | Points | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Presentation and defence** | 4 | Session 12, Wednesday 2 December |
| **Methods paper and repository** | 16 | Sunday 13 December 2026, 23:59 |
| | **20** | |

**The paper's rubric** — out of 100, scaled to its 16 points:

| | Weight |
|---|---|
| Question and contribution | 10 |
| Literature review (10 articles, organised by argument) | 15 |
| Data and provenance | 10 |
| Method — derived, not cited | 20 |
| Results and honest evaluation | 20 |
| Implementation note for R users — its example must run | 15 |
| Writing, references and statements | 10 |

- **The defence (4)** is judged on the clarity of the recommendation and on the answers of whichever
  member the instructor names.
- **The repository must run from a clean clone.** If it does not, the analysis cannot be marked.

### The individual multiplier — team work only

The 20 team points are multiplied by an individual factor, **×1.00 by default**.

| Your peer rating, relative to your group's mean | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 0.90 or above | **1.00** — no change |
| 0.75 to 0.90 | 0.90 |
| 0.60 to 0.75 | 0.80 |
| below 0.60 | 0.70 (the floor) |
| rated 4.5/5 or more, while a teammate falls below 1.00 | 1.05 |

**A multiplier below 1.00 is applied only if the git contribution history independently flags the
same student.** If the ratings and the history disagree, nothing changes automatically: the case
goes to the instructor with both records. The multiplier never touches the midterm, the final exam
or participation. Any student whose multiplier is not 1.00 is told why, and may ask for a review.

Details: [`team-work/`](team-work/README.md) · [`GROUP-ASSESSMENT.md`](../GROUP-ASSESSMENT.md#how-a-multiplier-is-computed).

## 6. Final exam — 40%

**An individual research paper on an international question of your own, using one technique from
the semester, and a 15-minute oral the day after it is due.**

| Part | Points | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Research paper**, 6,000–8,000 words, written alone | 25 | Sunday 13 December 2026, 23:59 |
| **Oral**, 15 minutes, no documents | 15 | Monday 14 December 2026 (to be confirmed) |
| | **40** | |

**The paper's rubric** — out of 100, scaled to its 25 points:

| | Weight |
|---|---|
| Question and contribution — specific, answerable, international | 15 |
| Literature review (10 articles, organised by argument) | 15 |
| Data and provenance | 10 |
| Method — one method, derived, defended against the obvious alternative | 25 |
| Results and honest evaluation | 20 |
| Writing, references, repository and statements | 15 |

**The oral** is roughly half on your paper and half on Sessions 02 to 11, and it is marked out of 15 in three
equal parts:

| | Points |
|---|---|
| **Command of your own method** — derive it, state its assumptions, say what breaks it | 5 |
| **Command of the course** — connect your paper to the methods you did not use | 5 |
| **Honesty under pressure** — concede precisely what cannot be claimed | 5 |

In English or French, your choice.

- A paper submitted **without an LLM-use statement is returned ungraded**.
- The individual multiplier **never** applies: both parts already measure you alone.

Details: [`final-exam/`](final-exam/README.md).

## 7. Rules that apply to every component

- **Late work** — the team paper and the final paper: **10% per day, to a maximum of three days**,
  after which the component is not accepted. Extensions for documented circumstances, requested
  **before** the deadline.
- **LLM use** is allowed, and must be **disclosed** in every deliverable: the tool, the version,
  what it was used for, and — in both papers — at least one instance where it was wrong and how you
  found out. **Undisclosed substitution is academic misconduct**, handled under HEC Montréal's
  regulations. [Policy](../SYLLABUS.md#8-working-with-a-language-model).
- **You answer for every word and number you submit**, including the ones an assistant produced.
