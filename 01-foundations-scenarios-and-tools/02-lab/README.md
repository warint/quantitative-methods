## Part 1 — The scenario (~45 min)

### Its architecture, first

| Part | Period | Epistemic status |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Jan 2025 – Jun 2026 | **Fact-based** — the observed record |
| Scenario | Aug 2026 – Mar 2031 | **Speculative** — a constructed path |
| Epilogue | Jun 2034 | **Speculative** — a constructed endpoint |

The seam between the first row and the second is the most interesting line in the document. We will
find it together and ask what changes as you cross it.

### The questions

1. **Where does the evidence stop and the story start?** Point to a paragraph. Defend the choice.
2. **Which claims could be checked in 2027?** Not "will it happen" — *what observable would move
   your belief*, and who publishes it?
3. **Whose scenario is it?** Written by a foundation, with named authors, at a moment. What would a
   different author have made the protagonists worry about?
4. **What is conspicuously absent?** A scenario is defined as much by what it does not imagine.
5. **If you believed it, what would you do differently on Monday?** If nothing, what is it for?

> **A scenario is a conditional object.** It says *if these things, then those things*. It is not a
> forecast and it does not carry a probability. Confusing the two is the first error this course
> exists to prevent — and the last, in Session 11, when you write your own governance file.

## Part 2 — What AI actually is (~45 min)

You have just installed a model on your laptop. Before the course starts using that word for twelve
weeks, we should agree what it denotes.

### The questions

1. **What is the thing you downloaded?** You pulled roughly four gigabytes. What is in the file?
2. **What is it doing when it answers?** Not metaphorically. What computation runs?
3. **Where did its competence come from, and where does it stop?** Why is it fluent about a library
   and unreliable about the version you have installed?
4. **Is the model in the *Europe 2031* scenario the same kind of object as the one on your laptop?**
   The scenario turns on capability. Whose capability, doing what?
5. **What in this course is "AI" and what is a hundred-year-old statistical method with a new
   name?** Sessions 02 to 05 are older than the term. Does that matter?

### The distinction to leave with

> **Prediction is not inference, and neither is understanding.**
>
> A model that predicts well may be useless for choosing a policy — that is Session 10. A model
> that fits the past perfectly may be worthless next year — that is Sessions 04 and 11. And a
> system fluent in a subject may hold no representation of it at all.

## How the room works

- **Everyone speaks.** Not everyone at length, but nobody silent for ninety minutes.
- **Positions are provisional.** Change your mind out loud; that is the demonstration, not the
  embarrassment.
- **Cite the text.** "Page 14 says" beats "I feel like".
- **Disagree with me.** Especially about what AI is. I hold views; they are not the answer key.

There is no right answer to any question above, and no mark attached to today. Participation is
recorded from Session 02, when the labs begin.
